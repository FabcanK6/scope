# Realistic evaluation notes for SCOPE.

# experienced clinical research professional (real-06 and real-07 come from a
# larger sample corpus that turned out to be 7 notes with swapped details).
# People's names are replaced with invented ones. They are long, formal and mostly "clean": most topics are
# mentioned only to confirm there is no problem, and findings are often
# corrected during the visit. Same format and risk rubric as handwritten.txt.
# Action items are only labelled where the note states an open one; this set mainly
# measures risk, issues and metadata.
# Relabelled by the CRA after review (2026-10-03): real-04 low -> medium (a pending IRB approval that blocks
# screening is a major essential-document gap) and real-06 low -> medium (consent on an outdated ICF version is
# a major deviation even when the subject is re-consented during the visit).

=== real-05
risk: high
issues: SAE_REPORTING
---
Monitor: [[Rachel Moore|MONITOR]], CCRC
Visit Type: [[Directed/For-Cause Monitoring Visit|VISIT_TYPE]]
Date: [[September 18, 2026|VISIT_DATE]]
Notes:
This unscheduled visit was triggered due to a delay in the site reporting a Serious Adverse Event (SAE) for Subject 002-44.
The subject was hospitalized for acute cholecystitis on August 30, 2026, but the site did not notify the Sponsor until September 12, 2026, violating the mandatory 24-hour protocol reporting window. I met face-to-face with the PI to conduct a root-cause analysis. The PI explained that the subject failed to notify the site coordinator immediately, and the hospital discharge summary was delayed.
I retrained both the PI and the primary SC on SAE definition and reporting timelines. The site has implemented a Corrective and Preventive Action (CAPA) plan, which includes adding a "Call the Study Team Immediately if Hospitalized" sticker to all subject diary cards. I verified that the eCRF SAE form matches the hospital discharge summary regarding dates, severity, and the PI's causality assessment (determined to be "Not Related" to the study drug).
